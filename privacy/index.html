Termen — Privacy Policy

Last updated 25 October 2026

Termen does not collect, transmit, or share any personal data with the developer or any third party. Everything the app sends or receives travels directly between your iPhone and your Mac through the WireGuard-encrypted Tailscale tunnel.

What Termen is

Termen ("the app") is an iPhone client that remote-controls a copy of cmux running on your own Mac, over your own Tailscale network.

What the app stores on your device

The following items are stored locally in the iOS Keychain or UserDefaults and never leave your device, except as part of the connection to your own Mac:

ItemWherePurpose
Tailscale host name / IPUserDefaults (cmux.host)Address of your own relay
Relay portUserDefaults (cmux.port)TCP port of your own relay (default 4399)
Bearer tokeniOS KeychainAuthenticates this iPhone to your relay
Device identifieriOS KeychainIdentifies this iPhone to your relay
Demo mode flagUserDefaults (cmux.demoMode)Whether App Review / first-run demo mode is on

The bearer token and device id are issued by your own Mac relay during the first connection. The developer of this app never sees them.

What the app sends over the network

When connected (i.e. demo mode is off and you have configured a host), the app sends:

  • A WebSocket connection (ws://<your-tailscale-host>:<your-port>/v1/ws) carrying the app's JSON-RPC protocol.
  • The same payloads you would type at your Mac terminal: text, key events, screen-subscribe requests.
  • Pictures you choose to attach. Your Mac saves each one under ~/.cache/cmux/task-attachments/.

These connections only terminate at the relay process (cmux-relay) running on the Mac you configured. They do not transit any server controlled by the app developer.

The NSAppTransportSecurity / NSAllowsArbitraryLoads exception declared in the app's Info.plist is required because the relay communicates over plain HTTP/WS — confidentiality and integrity are guaranteed by the underlying WireGuard tunnel that Tailscale provides between the two devices.

Notifications

The app uses UNUserNotificationCenter to display local notifications when the relay forwards events from cmux while the app is running. iOS asks for your permission first.

The App Store build gets local notifications only. When the app is closed, nothing arrives; the Inbox shows what you missed when you open it again.

The app also registers for remote notifications (APNs). A push to a closed app works only for a build you sign yourself, with an APNs key from your own Apple developer team: Apple accepts a key only for apps signed by the team that made it. This is what happens to the device token:

  • The token goes to your own relay on your own Mac, and nowhere else. It is sent over the same Tailscale tunnel as everything else, to POST /v1/devices/me/apns.
  • Your Mac stores it in ~/.termen/devices.json, readable only by your own account (0600).
  • Your relay sends the notification through Apple's servers using your own APNs key, which you supply in ~/.termen/relay.json. The developer has no key, no token, and no server in this path.
  • The payload carries a fixed sentence — "cmux needs attention" / "Open Termen to review this inbox item." — plus opaque ids (workspace, surface, notification, thread, request). It never carries terminal contents, and it never carries the title or text of the event itself. A lock screen is a public surface, so the app fetches the detail over your tailnet after you tap.
  • Remote push is off until you configure an apns block in relay.json. With no key, the relay sends nothing and the token is simply stored.

What the app does not do

  • No analytics, telemetry, crash reporting, or usage statistics
  • No advertising SDKs, no advertising identifiers, no IDFA access
  • No third-party service integrations (Firebase, Google, etc.)
  • No location, camera, microphone, or contacts access. Dictation is the iOS keyboard's own, so the app never gets the microphone
  • No photo library access. To attach a picture, iOS shows its own picker, and the app gets only the picture you choose
  • No background data collection
  • No tracking across apps or websites

Demo Mode

When you enable Demo Mode in Settings, the app uses entirely fabricated workspaces, terminal output, and notifications generated locally. No network connections are made.

Children

The app is rated 4+ and contains no objectionable content, advertising, or tracking. It is, however, a developer tool and not intended for children under the age that can meaningfully use a Unix terminal.

Changes to this policy

If this policy ever changes, the new version will be published on this page with a new date. There is no opt-in mechanism because there is no data collection to opt in or out of.

Contact

Questions about this policy or about privacy in Termen can be raised here: github.com/termen-app/termen-app.github.io/issues. Open an issue and it will be answered there.
